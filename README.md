# Superstore Sales & Profitability Analysis

## 1. Project Overview

This project analyzes the sales and profitability of a retail Superstore between 2014 and 2017.

The main objective is to understand sales performance, identify factors affecting profitability, detect loss-making products, and provide data-driven business recommendations.

## 2. Dataset Description

The dataset contains 9,994 order lines and 21 columns, including:

- Order Date and Ship Date
- Customer and Product Information
- Category and Sub-Category
- Sales, Quantity, Discount, and Profit
- Region and Segment

## 3. Business Questions

This analysis aims to answer the following questions:

- How did sales and profit evolve over time?
- Which categories and sub-categories generate the highest profits?
- How do discounts relate to profitability?
- Which regions and customer segments perform best?
- Who are the most profitable customers?
- Which products generate losses?
- What actions could improve overall profitability?

## 4. Tools & Technologies

- Python
- Pandas
- NumPy

- Seaborn
- Jupyter Notebook

## 5. Key Findings

- Total sales reached approximately **$2.30 million**.
- Total profit was approximately **$286,397**.
- Technology generated the highest sales and total profit among the three categories.
- Furniture had a relatively low profit margin of approximately 2.49%.
- The West region achieved the highest sales, profit, and profit margin.
- The Home Office segment had the highest profit margin.
- Some products generated significant losses, particularly in the Tables sub-category.
- A negative correlation was observed between discounts and profit, although this does not establish causation.

## 6. Business Recommendations

- Review discount policies, especially for products with recurring losses.
- Investigate pricing and cost structures for loss-making products.
- Monitor Furniture profitability, particularly Tables and Bookcases.
- Focus on both sales volume and profit margin when evaluating customer segments.
- Regularly monitor product-level profitability.
